Sample data centre power brief

A 100 MW data centre: Blyth, Ratcliffe-on-Soar or Didcot?

A worked example of our data centre power brief, built from real public data. There is no client. We picked three areas near transmission substations as examples of different kinds of location; nothing here says a data centre is planned at any of them. A real brief compares up to three sites of your choosing and arrives within seven working days.

20 November 2026 · Prepared by Benzene as a sample · A desk review of public registers and published network data. Not legal advice, an engineering study or a connection offer.

1. The short answer

  • Blyth. The busiest node of the three: 3,650 MW queued across 4 projects, all offshore wind or storage, dated 2029 to 2036. It also has the most generation queuing nearby, which matters if you want to talk about co-location or a private wire. Confirmed figures, Inferred reading.
  • Ratcliffe-on-Soar. The least crowded: 909.9 MW queued across 4 projects, dated 2028 to 2033, on a former coal station site that already has 225 MW of storage and gas turbines connected. Confirmed figures, Inferred reading.
  • Didcot. 1,579 MW connected and 1,179.9 MW queued across 6 entries, dated 2027 to 2037. The only one of the three with a Gate 2 project (300 MW). The nearest local substations are rated Red for new demand, apart from two 132 kV primaries. Confirmed

None of this says whether 100 MW of demand can connect at any of them. NESO's register lists generation and storage, not demand applications. That is a question for NESO and the network operator, and section 6 lists what to ask. Inferred

5. The rules that apply

  • Ofgem's proposed Data Centre Commitment Fee. £237,500 to £712,500 per MW, for projects above 40 MW on the transmission system, and above 40 MW on the distribution system where a Transmission Entry Assessment applies. Held from offer acceptance to energisation, returned if the project proceeds as planned and forfeited if it terminates or fails the requirements. Confirmed Ofgem, Curate consultation, 29 July 2026, paragraphs 4.1, 5.1 and 5.3. A proposal: the consultation closed on 16 September 2026 and Ofgem said it intends to decide later in 2026. We had not found a decision by 20 November 2026.
  • At 100 MW that would be £23.75m to £71.25m, the same at all three areas. Inferred 100 × £237,500 and 100 × £712,500.
  • The Data Centre Covenant. HM Treasury's policy paper of 28 October 2026 sets five tests. For "strategic" data centres that meet them, the government offers to reserve and reallocate grid connections, identify additional capacity and accelerate connections before 2030. "Strategic data centre" is not yet defined and no MW threshold is given. Confirmed GOV.UK, The Data Centre Covenant.

6. Questions to put to NESO and the network operator

  1. Is there a demand connection point at this substation for 100 MW, and at what voltage?
  2. What demand is already contracted or applied for at this node? The register will not tell us.
  3. What reinforcement works are needed, and what are their planned dates?
  4. Would a non-firm or flexible connection be offered earlier than a firm one?
  5. Would the 40 MW fee threshold apply to the project as one application, or stage by stage?
