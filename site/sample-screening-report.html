A 100 MW battery near Case Study Site A

A worked example of our £1,950 screening report, built from real public data. There is no client. The site is an invented 100 MW battery near a 400kV transmission substation in Northern Powergrid's area, which we call Case Study Site A (Site A from here on). Other projects in the queue are Site B, Site C and so on. A real report names the substation and the projects, covers your own site, and arrives within five working days.

21 November 2026 · Prepared by Benzene as a sample · A desk review of public registers and published network data. Not legal advice, an engineering study or a connection offer.

1. Summary

A new 100 MW battery at Site A would join behind 6,555 MW of unbuilt projects at the 400kV substation. None of them holds Gate 2, the earliest contracted date is October 2033, one battery ahead in the queue had its date moved three times in four weeks, and the local network shows no spare capacity at all.

Four findings that matter most

  1. The queue is long and none of it is Gate 2. Seven unbuilt projects totalling 6,555 MW are contracted to connect directly at the Site A 400kV substation. Three hold Gate 1 (4,400 MW) and four have a blank Gate field (2,155 MW). Their dates run from 30 October 2033 to 30 October 2035. Confirmed
  2. Dates here move, and not always in one direction. A 300 MW battery at the substation (Site B) went from 31 October 2033 to 30 October 2035, then to 31 October 2031, then back to 30 October 2035, across four weekly copies in October and November 2026. Reported
  3. The local network is full too. Northern Powergrid shows 0 MW of generation headroom and 0 of demand headroom at the Site A grid supply point and at all 19 substations below it, and rates the transmission side red for known National Grid constraints. Confirmed
  4. Staying in the queue may soon cost money. Ofgem has proposed a battery commitment fee from £3,000 per MW, rising to a maximum of £25,000 per MW. On 100 MW that is £300,000 at the start and up to £2.5m at the cap. It is a proposal, not a decision. Confirmed fee, Inferred arithmetic.

Three things to check before relying on a date here

  1. Ask National Grid Electricity Transmission and NESO what reinforcement works the Site A dates depend on, and whether a new Gate 2 application would be assessed before or after the existing Gate 1 entries.
  2. Ask Northern Powergrid when the transmission constraint at the grid supply point is expected to lift, and whether a flexible or non-firm distribution connection is on offer in the meantime.
  3. Ask the landowner and your lawyer whether the option long-stop date allows for a connection date in the mid-2030s.
