1. The short answer

The transmission queue here is long. 7,578 MW is queued at Creyke Beck 400kV across 6 projects, none of it holding Gate 2, with contracted dates running from 2028 to 2039. Confirmed

The local network looks different. Northern Powergrid shows 323 MW of generation headroom at its Creyke Beck grid supply point, rated Green, but rates the nearest 33 kV and most nearby 11 kV substations Red for new generation. Confirmed

A large scheme needing a transmission connection here would join the back of a long queue. A smaller scheme on the local network would be judged mainly on Northern Powergrid's capacity, and the nearest substations are the constrained ones. Only a connection offer settles either. Inferred

4. What this means for an option

Facts first, then our reading. None of this is advice on option terms.

  • Gate 2 land test. NESO's guidance says an option agreement used as Gate 2 land evidence needs a minimum term of three years from the date it was signed, unless an exception applies. Confirmed NESO's lease pro forma sets a default lease term of 20 years from the date the option is exercised. Reported
  • Timing. Contracted dates at Creyke Beck run to 2039. If a developer's plan depends on a new transmission connection here, its date could fall after a three to five year option has run. Ask how the two line up. Inferred
  • Local headroom. The grid supply point shows headroom; the nearer 33 kV and 11 kV substations are mostly rated Red. Ask which one the developer would connect to.

5. Questions to put to the developer

  1. Which substation do you plan to connect to, and do you hold a connection offer or have you applied?
  2. If you hold an offer, what is its Gate status and its connection date?
  3. Is the plan a transmission connection at Creyke Beck, or a local connection through Northern Powergrid?
  4. If local, what has Northern Powergrid said about the Red ratings at Beverley and the nearby 11 kV substations?
  5. What happens to the option payments if the connection date moves?
