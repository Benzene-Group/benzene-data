How we work out a realistic connection date

Learned from our own copies of NESO's register, 6 Dec 2024 to 17 Nov 2026.

A contracted connection date is a promise, not a forecast. So we checked how those promises have actually held up. We took every project waiting to connect in our earliest copy of the register, grouped them by technology and by how far away their contracted date was, and looked at where each one stood two years later: same date, moved later, moved earlier, or gone from the register.

For any project today, we find its group and report three things. Date risk: High if half or more of similar projects moved a month or more later, Medium from a quarter, otherwise Low. Likely date: the contracted date plus the typical move, when the risk is High; otherwise the contracted date. Dropout: the share of similar projects that left the register. Where a group is small, we use all technologies together.

The pattern is clear. Dates within two years slip far more often than dates further out, so a project's risk rises as its date gets closer.
